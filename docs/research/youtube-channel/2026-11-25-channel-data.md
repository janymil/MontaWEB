# YouTube niche scan: channel data (checked 2026-11-25)

## Method
- Real browser (Claude's built-in browser pane, logged out). YouTube search with filter "this month, sorted by view count" for 19 queries in English and Spanish. That surfaces the videos that won in the last ~4 weeks, whoever made them.
- For channels that looked new or faceless: About panel (join date, subscribers, video count, total views) and the Videos tab (views of the last 10 uploads).
- The browser profile is German, so YouTube showed some titles auto-translated into German. Numbers are not affected.
- **Not verified for any channel:** whether it is monetized (not visible from outside), and whether it is AI-made (I did not watch the videos; "AI signals" below come from title templates, upload rate and handle pattern only). Social Blade, Playboard and vidIQ were not used.
- Sample bias: search by views shows winners. Channels that started and got nothing are invisible here. The "median of last 10" column is the honest number for "what a typical upload gets".

## Reading of the data
1. New faceless history/ancient-civilization channels break out in **both** English and Spanish right now. Several channels under 2 months old already have 7–27k subscribers.
2. The distribution is very skewed: a typical new channel's median upload gets 500–13,000 views, and one or two videos carry the channel. EL ORIGEN (median ~124k) is the exception and the best-in-class example.
3. AI drama channels grow subscribers fast, but their current median uploads are low (FlashCut ~640). Their output is 45–120 min stories with consistent characters, which a 5–10 h/week solo creator can't produce.
4. Reddit stories and Spanish horror are led by human personalities and high-cadence veterans; the data shows no opening for a new AI channel.
5. True-crime bodycam channels are booming, but they are built on reused police footage, not on AI.
